Interpretation

The six variable-importance measures produce broadly consistent results. Across most methods, impact, trust, and service emerge as the strongest predictors of customer satisfaction.

Among all variables, impact receives the highest Shapley value and Johnson relative weight, indicating that it contributes the most to explaining variation in satisfaction. This result suggests that customers who perceive the brand as having a meaningful impact tend to report higher satisfaction levels.

Trust also ranks highly across multiple measures, including Pearson correlation, standardized regression coefficients, and random forest importance. The consistency of these results highlights the central role of trust in shaping customer satisfaction.

Similarly, service performs strongly across nearly all methods, suggesting that customer experience remains an important determinant of satisfaction.

At the other end of the ranking, variables such as popular, rewarding, and build receive comparatively lower importance scores. While these factors are positively related to satisfaction, they appear to contribute less to explaining differences in satisfaction than impact, trust, and service.

Overall, the results suggest that customer satisfaction is driven primarily by perceptions of impact, trust, and service quality, with other brand attributes playing a more limited role.
